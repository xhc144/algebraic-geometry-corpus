2.3.2. The fpqce topology. It is sometimes useful to consider coverings that
are not locally finitely presented. One can define a topology on (Sch/S') simply by
taking all collections of morphisms {U; — U} such that the resulting morphism
LL, Ui — U is faithfully flat. Unfortunately, this topology is not well behaved (see
Remarks 1.14 and 2.56). One needs some finiteness condition in order to get a
reasonable topology.

For example, one could define a covering as a collection of morphisms {U; > U}
such that the resulting morphism {]]; U; — U} is faithfully flat and quasi-compact,
as I did in the first version of these notes; but then Zariski covers would not be
included, and the resulting topology would not be comparable with the Zariski
topology. The definition of the fpqe topology that follows, suggested by Steve
Kleiman, gives the correct sheaf theory.
