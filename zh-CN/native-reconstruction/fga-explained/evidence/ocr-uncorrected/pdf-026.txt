14 2. CONTRAVARIANT FUNCTORS

DEFINITION 2.1. A representable functor on the category C is a functor
F; CP — (Set)

which is isomorphic to a functor of the form hx for some object X of C.
If this happens, we say that F' is represented by X.

Given two isomorphisms F' ~ hy and F ~ hy, we have that the resulting
isomorphism hy ~ hy comes from a unique isomorphism X ~ Y in C, because of
the weak form of Yoneda’s lemma. Hence two objects representing the same functor
are canonically isomorphic.

One way to think about Yoneda’s lemma is as follows. The weak form says that
the category C is embedded in the category Hom(C°?, (Set)). The strong version
says that, given a functor F': C°P — (Set), this can be extended to the representable
functor hr: Hom(C°?, (Set))°? — (Set): thus, every functor become representable,
when extended appropriately. (In practice, the category Hom(C°?, (Set)) is usually
much too big, and one has to restrict it appropriately.)

We can use Yoneda’s lemma to give a very important characterization of rep-
